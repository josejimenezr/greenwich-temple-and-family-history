Temple & Family History

Ward temple and family history coordination meeting: who attends, what it focuses on, and how the ward plan is built, as outlined in the General Handbook.

Purpose of the Meeting

General Handbook 25.2.7, Ward Temple and Family History Coordination Meetings

Topics the meeting usually covers

  • How to implement the ward temple and family history plan1
  • How to help specific ward members prepare to receive their temple ordinances1
  • How to help specific ward members with their temple and family history efforts1

Focus of the meeting

  • The needs of individuals, especially youth, new and returning members, and those who have not been endowed1

Who attends

  • Ward temple and family history leader (conducts)
  • Assigned members of the Relief Society, elders quorum, and Primary presidencies
  • An assistant in the priests quorum (or the teachers or deacons quorum president if no priests)
  • A presidency member of the oldest Young Women class
  • Temple and family history consultants

All who attend counsel together as equal participants.1

Creating the Ward Temple & Family History Plan

General Handbook 25.2.6, Ward Temple and Family History Plan · see also 25.2.5, Ward Council
"Each ward should have a simple temple and family history plan." General Handbook 25.2.6
✓The plan could include
  • Helping specific members understand the blessings of worshipping in the house of the Lord.
  • Helping members prepare to receive temple ordinances.
  • Helping members use Ordinances Ready to take an ancestor's name to the temple, and use FamilySearch to add what they know about deceased relatives who need ordinances. Ordinances Ready: see 25.4.1, Online Resources.
  • Involving new and returning members, youth, and children in temple and family history work. Family Name Assist is a simple way to help these members prepare family names (25.4.1).
  • Helping Primary children prepare to receive a temple recommend and to worship in the house of the Lord regularly where possible. The Primary presidency should hold a Temple and Priesthood Preparation Meeting each year (see 12.1.7 and 12.2.4).
✕The plan should not
  • Establish quotas for temple worship.
  • Establish reporting systems for temple worship.

"The ward temple and family history plan should not establish quotas or reporting systems for temple worship." (25.2.6)

Ideas for the Updated Ward Plan

Summary of the Temple & Family History Leader's draft proposal (Aug 2026)
The shift

From running a programme to helping a few people at a time

A simple, repeating cycle

  1. 1IdentifyPrayerfully find a few people who could be blessed now
  2. 2HelpOne-to-one help with one achievable next step
  3. 3Connect to the templeGo with them or prepare a family name together
  4. 4Follow upAsk what it meant and choose the next step

Continue, simplify, stop, start

✓Continue
  • One-to-one help: preparing a family name, printing cards, using Ordinances Ready
  • Ordinances Ready, Family Name Assist and the ward FamilySearch group
  • Sharing local spiritual stories; youth involved in existing temple visits
−Simplify
  • One short monthly message instead of a full newsletter
  • One ward-supported temple opportunity per quarter
  • Brief coordination conversations and one annual emphasis
✕Stop
  • Numeric participation targets and quota-like goals
  • "All members" language that sounds compulsory
  • Repeating every event each year regardless of impact
+Start
  • A small confidential list of individuals being helped
  • One owner and one clear next step per person
  • Personal follow-up after temple visits and a quarterly review of what helps
